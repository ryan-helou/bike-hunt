## Fit (5'9", 175 cm, cycling inseam 81.5 cm, target saddle height ~72 cm BB to top)
Ryan is fine going a bit bigger or smaller (2026-09-27), so the range is wide on purpose.
- **Ideal:** 54 cm / Medium.
- **Fine:** 51–56 cm, S/M through M/L.
  - **Smaller** (51–53) is the safer direction on a TT bike: a lower front end is more aero, and the seatpost and stem make up the difference. Check that at least ~72 cm of saddle height is reachable.
  - **Bigger** (55–56) is fine if stack is about 530 or less and reach is about 440 or less. Too tall a front end can't be fixed.
- **No:** 50 and under, XS, 650c wheels, 57+ / L and up, or a standover height at or above 81.5 cm.
- If the ad gives no size, ask for it. Never assume.

### Videos: always watch them (Ryan, 2026-09-30)
**Any listing with a video: always play it and screenshot frames**, even when there are photos too. Videos often show what the photos hide: the drivetrain side, damage, the decals, and how the seller rides it.
1. Click the video. If it spins, click play and wait up to about 10 s. Bring the tab to the front, because background tabs often won't load video.
2. Take 4–6 screenshots spread over the video. Pause on the decals and model name, the drivetrain, the brakes, the cockpit and the frame junctions. Zoom where it helps.
3. Record what the frames show in the notes: "video: …".
4. If the video still won't load, say so in the notes and ask the seller for photos. Never judge a bike from the ad text alone.
5. Check every still photo too.

### Size by brand and model: the label on the frame isn't enough
Brands measure differently. A "54" Cervélo, a "54" Felt and a "Medium" Speed Concept are not the same bike. For every candidate:
1. Identify the exact model and year. Use the photos (decals, cable routing, brakes, fork) if the ad doesn't say.
2. Look up that model and year's **stack and reach** for the stated size in the manufacturer's geometry chart. Good sources are geometrygeeks.bike, bikeinsights.com, 99spokes.com, or an archived manufacturer PDF.
3. Judge on stack and reach, not the label.

**Ryan's window:**
- **Stack:** 480–530 mm (best 495–520).
- **Reach:** 395–445 mm (best 410–435).

A lower stack or a shorter reach is fixable with spacers, a stem and the aerobar pads. A stack that's too high isn't fixable. If you can't find the geometry, ask the seller to measure (centre of the BB to the top of the head tube), or ask for their height. A seller between 5'7" and 5'11" is a good sign.

**Starting points only:** confirm with the chart every time.

## Hard rules for the bike
- Rim or disc brakes are both fine, but **standard calipers only** (direct-mount counts as standard). **Proprietary or integrated brakes/fronts = pass:** Trek Speed Concept 2011–2017 (**both** Gen 1 and Gen 2), Specialized Shiv 2011–2019 (except maybe the alloy 2012–13 Shiv Elite A1) and **Transition carbon 2008–2012** (centre-pull, rear under the BB; alloy Transitions are fine), Giant Trinity Advanced/Pro 2016+ (SpeedControl), BMC TM01, Argon E-118, Felt IA/DA, Cannondale Slice RS, Scott Plasma 5, Speedmax CF SLX.
- **Recalls to check:** Felt 2010 B12/B14/B16 fork steerer (confirm the replacement fork), Cervélo P3 Ultegra 3T base bar sold Sep 2012–Jan 2013, Slice RS 2013 brake plates.
- Bottom brackets: threaded BSA (easiest) on P1, P2 2010–14, P3 2010–13, Argon E-112/114. BBright press-fit on P2 2015+ and P3 2013/14+. BB30 on the Slice (creaks; budget $60–120). BB90 on the Speed Concept.
- Rim brakes need an **alloy braking surface**. Carbon clincher rims with rim brakes are a no (descents).
- Rear axle: 130 mm QR, or 142 thru-axle, which the trainer can take with an adapter.
- Groupset: at least Sora/Tiagra-level shifting, preferably 105/Ultegra/Rival/Force. Note whether it's 10- or 11-speed.
- **Fake wheels:** "HED/Zipp/Enve" decals on Novatec or unbranded hubs. Ask for a photo of the hub.
- Area: about 3 h or less from H7T 2W7, in Canada only. That covers Montréal, Laurentides, Lanaudière, Montérégie, Estrie, Trois-Rivières, Québec City (~2.75 h), Ottawa-Gatineau (~2 h), Kingston (~2.75 h) and Cornwall. It excludes Toronto/GTA and the US.

## Valuing a bike: use real numbers, not guesses
The table above is only a starting point. For every candidate that might be a good deal, set `fairValue` from evidence:
1. **Bicycle Blue Book** (bicyclebluebook.com/value-guide): look up the brand, model and year. Take the "Good" condition used value, convert USD to CAD (about 1.42 as of Sep 2026), then take off about 10% for the Québec/Ontario private market.
2. **Comparables:** find 2–4 similar listings on Marketplace (same model ±2 years, similar groupset) and on Pinkbike's buy/sell (pinkbike.com/buysell, road/tri). Asking prices run high, so count them at about 85%.
3. **Wheels on their own:** if the bike has real deep carbon wheels (Zipp, HED, Enve, Reynolds, Mavic Cosmic Carbone), value them separately with Pinkbike or eBay sold listings, then add them to the frameset value.
4. Put `fairValue` as the lower end of what the evidence supports. In notes, write the sources in one line, for example: "BBB US$1,050 → ~C$1,300; comps $1,400/$1,550 (P2 2012 105)".

## All-in cost: count the real price, not the sticker
The good-deal check compares resale value with the **all-in cost**, not the asking price. All-in cost is the likely closing price plus:
- **Gas for the pickup:** about $25 per hour of one-way driving. Ottawa ≈ $50 and Québec City ≈ $70 round trip.
- **No tune-up cost (Ryan, 2026-09-30: "who says I'll go to a tune-up").** He maintains the bike himself, so don't add a shop tune-up or a shop build. Only count parts that actually need replacing.
- **Parts that need replacing, based on the photos and the ad:**
  - tyres: $100 for a pair of GP5000s if they're cracked, square or Gatorskins
  - chain: $40
  - cassette: $60–90 if the chain is visibly worn or the bike has high km
  - bar tape and base-bar tape: $30
  - brake pads: $30
  - cables and housing: $40
- **Anything missing:** a saddle ($60 used), bottle cages, and aerobar pads ($40).
- Pedals are not a cost, because Ryan moves his own.

Put the total in `allInCost` and list what went into it in notes. **The good-deal check uses `allInCost`**: fair used value must be at least the all-in cost.

## Red flags in photos: check every candidate
Zoom into the photos and ask for more if the view is missing. Record anything you find in notes:
- **Carbon:**
  - cracks or chips near the dropouts, the seat-tube clamp, the steerer and top-cap area, the BB, and under the down tube (chainsuck damage behind the chainrings)
  - paint cracking in a straight line
  - clear coat that's bubbled or lifted
- **Alloy frames** (P1/P2 alloy, Transition, Felt B alloy):
  - **stuck seatpost:** corrosion where the post enters the frame, or white powder at the clamp. This is common on old alloy frames with an alloy or carbon post, and it's very expensive to fix. Ask the seller whether the seatpost moves.
  - cracks at the welds, especially around the BB and the head tube
- **Rims:** a concave brake track (a worn-out rim; check the wear indicator), or rust spots on the spokes.
- **Drivetrain:**
  - hooked or shark-fin chainring teeth
  - a rusted chain
  - bar-end shifters with a broken ratchet ("shifts only in friction")
- **Parts you can't replace:**
  - proprietary brakes (Shiv 2011+, Speed Concept 1st gen, P4/P5 early)
  - integrated stems or fronts
  - 9-speed Dura-Ace bar-ends: they work, but cost more to replace
  - old Di2 without a charger
- **Signs of a crash:**
  - a bent rear derailleur hanger (the derailleur isn't parallel to the cassette)
  - scuffed bar-end shifters or aerobar ends
  - a replaced fork of a different make
- A bike with no clear photos of the drivetrain side is a question to ask, not a pass.

## Good deal or not (Ryan, 2026-09-29: replaces the old steal test)
Ryan doesn't want a steal score. For every candidate, give a plain verdict: **Good deal** or **Not a good deal**, with one line of why.

A listing is a **good deal** when all of these hold:
1. It fits and passes every hard rule.
2. It can likely close at **$1,200 or less**.
3. Its **all-in cost is at or below its fair used value**: he'd get his money back if he resold it. (See "Valuing a bike" and "All-in cost".)

Anything else is **not a good deal**, even a nice bike: say which point fails (too small, proprietary brakes, all-in above value, too far…). Put the verdict first in `verdict`, then the numbers, for example "Good deal: worth ~$1,400; all-in ~$1,270 (close $1,100 + gas $50 + tune-up $120)". A good deal with a big margin (value well above all-in) is worth saying so, but no separate score.

Where good deals come from:
- **Underpriced by people who don't know the bike:** vague titles like "vélo de course", "vélo de mon mari", "bike for sale"; moving or estate sales; spouses selling. Photos showing a Cervélo P3/P2 carbon, Speed Concept, Argon, Felt DA/B2, or Zipp/Enve/HED wheels on real hubs.
- **Stale listings from motivated sellers:** a bike worth $1,600–2,200 that has sat for weeks at $1,500–1,900. These can come down to about $1,100–1,200 with a polite, reasoned offer.
- **Race wheels bundled with the bike:** real Zipp 404/808, HED Jet or Enve are worth $600–1,200 on their own, so a "bike + deep wheels" ad can be a good deal even near full price.

**Missing parts are fine (Ryan, 2026-09-29).** A frameset, a bike with no wheels, or one missing a saddle, aerobars or a shifter can still be a good deal: price the missing parts at used Québec prices (a rim-brake 10/11-speed wheelset ~$150–350 alloy or ~$300–450 FLO/Cosmic Carbone-type; 105 group ~$250–400; aerobars ~$80–150; saddle ~$60) add them to `allInCost`, and judge on that. Only proprietary parts you can't buy (Speed Concept/Shiv brakes, a broken Plasma seat-mast topper) still mean pass.

**Buying priorities:** frame and fit first, then standard parts, then groupset (105/Ultegra; replacing a group costs $300–500+). Wheels are the easy swap: stock wheels resell for ~$50–150.

**Message anything with potential, not only good deals (Ryan, 2026-09-30).** A listing gets an opener draft when it's **Good deal** or **Potential**. Potential means none of the three points is clearly failing yet:
- It fits, or the size is unknown but plausible (ask).
- It passes the hard rules, or the ad doesn't say (e.g. brakes or year unknown: ask).
- A good-deal price is realistic with negotiation. Stale listings, price drops and listings up to about $2,000 count, as long as the fair value is at or above ~$1,000.

In practice: any tri/TT bike in range that isn't a clear pass and hasn't been messaged yet gets a short opener. The opener asks what the ad doesn't say plus the best cash price. Only name a committed number ("if you can do $X, it's a deal") when $X is a good-deal price.

Also draft a nudge for any seller who hasn't replied in 3+ days, at most one nudge per listing.

List every Potential draft in the notification and the final message with a one-line reason ("P2 carbon, size unknown, 6 weeks listed"). They still need Ryan's "send N" / "send all", like every message.

Everything else that fits goes to `watching`, and anything that fails a rule goes to `passed` with the reason. Watch for price drops on tracked listings, since a drop can turn a `watching` listing into a good deal.

## Scam signals (mark passed)
Any of these gets a listing marked passed:
- Shipping only, or "I'm away, the courier will bring it"
- A deposit or e-transfer before viewing
- Stock photos, or a price far below every comparable
- Generic emoji-template text
- The seller refuses a test ride

**Not worth it (numbers checked 2026-09-28):**

- **Europe:** buycycle doesn't ship to Canada, and the classified sites are domestic only. Landed cost is about 1.5× the euro price (EUR→CAD 1.61). Canyon's cheapest Speedmax in Canada is C$7,599.
- **China:** a frameset lands at $1,400–2,000. Built up, it's $2,900–4,300. Rim versions use hidden proprietary brakes, and resale is weak.
- **New bikes from Canadian shops:** the cheapest new tri bike is about $3,500 (Aquila Crono 105), and prior-year Cervélo P / Argon E-117 run $5,300+. Clearance season runs October to February, but it's still far over budget.
- **Building one:** a used frameset plus used parts plus a shop build comes to about $1,650–2,700, and 11-speed 105 was discontinued in March 2026. It only ties a $1,000 complete bike if a fitting frameset turns up at $400 or less and a used 105 11-speed group at $250 or less.

## Negotiation playbook
- **Language:** reply in the seller's language. Use French for a French ad. Keep it short, friendly and plain, as Ryan would text.
- **Photos first, fewest questions (Ryan, 2026-09-30).** Before writing any message, open every photo of the listing and zoom in: model and year from the livery, brake type, frame material, groupset, wheels, condition. Drop every question the ad or the photos already answer, and never repeat a question an earlier message in the thread already asked (a follow-up carries only the offer). Prefer a committed offer that depends on the one unknown ("if it's a 51, $850 and it's a deal") over a list of questions. The aim is a smooth thread with as few back-and-forths as possible.
- **Opening message:** ask only the questions the ad and photos don't answer, plus the lowest price. The questions are:
  - frame size
  - crashes or cracks
  - groupset, and 10 or 11 speed
  - wheel and hub make
  - the seller's height
  - the best price for cash
- **First offer:** about 70–75% of asking. Always give concrete reasons: a 10-speed groupset, worn tape and pads, tyres to replace, weeks on the market, comparable listings.
- **Walking up:** move in small steps and never go above `target`. Stop after 3 counters; the bike becomes `watching` and you check back if the price drops.
- **Tone (Ryan, 2026-09-29): sound like a committed, excited buyer.** Don't write "subject to seeing it" / "sous réserve de le voir" in messages; say he's ready to take it and "if you can do $X, it's a deal". Ryan still inspects at pickup (checklist under Locking) and can walk away if the bike isn't as described.
- **Only commit at a good-deal price (Ryan, 2026-09-29).** Never offer or accept, in committed language ("it's a deal"), a price at which the bike isn't a good deal (all-in above value, or a fit doubt). On 2026-09-29 the agent agreed $1,100 on a too-small Slice whose all-in was above its value, and Ryan had to cancel. Openers and questions are fine; the committed number must be a good deal.
- **Never** offer or agree to a deposit, a shipment, or an e-transfer before viewing. Never share Ryan's address or phone number.
- **Handing off:** once there's a price, the viewing time is Ryan's call. The seller only has to confirm the number; logistics go to Ryan.

### How to open (Ryan, 2026-09-30: "open with less")
About 30% of sellers replied. The silent ones mostly got openers with 4–6 questions, or lowballs on overpriced bikes.
- **Opener = "still available?" plus at most ONE question**, whichever matters most: usually the frame size, otherwise the year or the groupset. Don't ask what the photos or ad already answer. Ask further questions only after the seller replies.
- **Overpriced listings** (asking more than about 1.4× fair value): don't open with a number. Ask whether they're flexible on price and let them name one. Once they reply, Ryan's aggressive offers are fine; he wants to keep making them.
- **Follow-ups:** one nudge about 3 days after our last message, then stop. That listing goes back to `watching`, and we only contact it again if the price drops.
- **Toronto/GTA and anything over 3 h:** only for an exceptional deal (margin $400+). Never "let's meet partway" otherwise.

**Opener examples**
- French: "Salut! Toujours disponible? C'est quelle grandeur de cadre?"
- English: "Hi! Is this still available? What frame size is it?"
- For an overpriced listing: "Salut! Toujours disponible? Es-tu flexible sur le prix?"

### When to make an offer (Ryan, 2026-09-30: "don't send offers for no reason, but don't be scared to send offers")
Having several firm offers out at once is fine; Ryan doesn't mind. But every offer needs a reason:
- **The seller answered**, and the bike fits and is worth more than the offer. Make the offer in the same reply.
- **The seller has been quiet for about 36 h or more** after our questions. A concrete number is the nudge.
- **The price is so low that speed wins** (a possible mispricing). Offer right away.
- **Don't follow up within about 24 h of our last message.** Don't send offers on bikes whose fit or basic facts are still unknown, unless the offer is explicitly conditional on fit. Don't send offers that only reach a fair price, not a good deal.

### Message examples (match this tone: short, friendly, specific, written like Ryan)
**Opening, French:**
> Salut! Toujours disponible? C'est quelle grandeur de cadre exactement, et t'es de quelle taille toi? Est-ce que le vélo a déjà été accidenté? Et c'est quoi ton meilleur prix cash? Je suis à Laval, je peux passer cette semaine.

**Opening, English:**
> Hi! Is this still available? What frame size is it exactly, and how tall are you? Any crashes? What's the best you could do for cash? I'm in Laval and can come by this week.

**Counter, French** (always give reasons that are true for this bike):
> Merci pour les infos! Vu que c'est du 10 vitesses et qu'il faudrait changer les pneus et la chaîne, je peux t'offrir 950$ cash, sous réserve de l'essayer. Ça te va?

**Counter, English:**
> Thanks! Since it's 10-speed and would need new tyres and a chain, I could do $950 cash, subject to a test ride. Would that work?

**Locking, French:**
> Parfait, 1 050$ ça marche, sous réserve de le voir et l'essayer. Je te reviens pour un moment qui te convient.

- One question burst, then wait.
- Never more than about 4 lines.
- No emojis.
- Never lie about Ryan or the bike.
- Never pressure with fake deadlines or fake other offers.
